This case is dedicated to the 200th & 515th Coast Artillery Anti-Aircraft Corps. Between April 9 and April 17, 1942, the Imperial Japanese Army forced roughly 75,000 American and Filipino prisoners of war on a 65-mile march from Mariveles to Camp O'Donnell on the Bataan Peninsula. The prisoners received almost no food or water, and thousands did not survive. They endured severe physical abuse, beatings, and torture. The Bataan Death March struck close to home in Deming: many of the American soldiers captured at Bataan came from New Mexico. The 200th & 515th Coast Artillery units numbered 1,816 men total—829 died in battle, in captivity, or shortly after liberation, and 987 survived. Since 1989, the Bataan Memorial Death March—a marathon-length, 26.2-mile march and run—has been held each spring at White Sands Missile Range in their memory. The cabinets around you hold newspaper articles and photographs donated by Deming-area POWs and their families. The large, framed plaque in the right-hand cabinet lists 83 Luna County residents who served in the 200th & 515th Coast Artillery; thirty of them were killed in action. Nearby is a shadowbox donated by the family of Lee Gardner.
