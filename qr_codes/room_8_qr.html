This room may be difficult for some visitors, as it illustrates the medical practices and equipment in everyday use. The items were donated by the doctors and the hospitals that served Deming from the 1930s through the 1950s. Among the most striking pieces are two iron lungs — an adult version and an infant/child — that were used during the polio epidemics of 1949 and 1952. The Women's Hospital donated them to the Deming Memorial Hospital and funds were raised by the Deming Rotary Club to purchase and then donate them to the Museum. You'll also find a wooden birth chair from Spain, along with artifacts from hospitals and clinics throughout the Deming area, including the Women's Hospital, Faywood Hot Springs, the Camp Cody Red Cross Building and Infirmary, and Holy Cross Hospital, which operated for many years as a tuberculosis sanitarium. The nun doll on display was donated by the Holy Cross Order of South Bend, Indiana, along with a research paper detailing the order's history establishing and running the Holy Cross Sanitarium in Deming. Medicine has come a long way since these tools were in daily use.
