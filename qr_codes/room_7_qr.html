Transportation has shaped life in Deming and throughout the Southwest for generations. From wagons and railroads to automobiles and modern highways, each advancement changed how people traveled, worked, traded, and stayed connected. On March 18, 1881, the Santa Fe and Southern Pacific Railroad met to create the southern transcontinental railroad line, joining east and west with a silver spike. Charles Crocker was one of the four railroad barons, and Deming was named after his wife, Mary Deming-Crocker. Deming boasted one of the grandest Harvey Houses in the Southwest—America's first restaurant chain—where single young women of good character, known as Harvey Girls, served gourmet meals on silver and fine china. Our exhibit preserves original lumber and paint from Deming's historic Harvey House, donated by Santa Fe employees. You'll also see the original sign from Deming's historic train station, Deming's first volunteer fire truck (go ahead and ring the bell or turn the siren handle!), the 1904 REO—the very first automobile owned by a Deming resident—and two classic soap box derby cars raced in El Paso.
