Welcome to the Military Room. Along the left wall are photographs of Luna County residents who served in the various branches of our Armed Forces, and the cabinets around you hold insignia, uniforms, mementos, and challenge coins donated and loaned by local families. Look for the faded black-and-white photo of the 200th and 515th Coast Artillery — the New Mexico servicemen who would later take part in the Bataan Death March. A full exhibit on the march is located near the museum's entrance. World War II In the next room, you'll find uniforms, insignia, and mementos donated and loaned by WWII veterans and their families, along with several scale models of the ships and planes of the era. At the center of the room, a plexiglass case holds a Norden Mark XV Bombsight — the same model installed and tested in the B-17s that trained right here at the Deming Army Airfield. Beginning in March 1943, a new class of 150 to 200 bombardier students graduated every three weeks, and by the war's end nearly 8,000 bombardiers had trained in Deming. The Norden was considered revolutionary: for the first time, a mechanical computer was paired with an autopilot to promise real gains in bombing accuracy. Deming's airmen spent the rest of the war chasing that promise — and by the time the fighting ended, it still hadn't quite been kept. Fort Cummings Room This room is devoted to Fort Cummings, the first walled fort in the New Mexico Territory, garrisoned by the U.S. Cavalry from October 1863 until its abandonment in 1873. You can still trace the outline of its walls on the southeast side of Cooke's Peak, just west of Highway 26. For years, the fort was a regular stop on the Butterfield Overland Mail route. The cases here feature the uniforms, insignia, and artwork of the 9th and 10th Cavalry Regiments — the famed "Buffalo Soldiers." Camp Cody During World War I, Deming hosted one of several training camps built across the Southwest to take advantage of its sunshine and mild winters. Camp Cody — named for William "Buffalo Bill" Cody of Wild West Show fame — trained National Guard units from North and South Dakota, Nebraska, Minnesota, and Iowa. The camp opened on July 16, 1916, and closed in early 1919, not long after the Armistice of November 11, 1918, brought the war to an end. Many of its barracks, buildings, and mess halls were sold off afterward and are scattered throughout Luna County today. The camp hospital had a second life of its own: the Sisters of the Holy Cross later purchased it and ran it as a tuberculosis sanitarium through the 1920s and '30s. Pancho Villa Raid On March 9, 1916, Pancho Villa crossed into the United States and raided Columbus, New Mexico, killing several people. The Deming unit of the National Guard — whose flag is on display here — was dispatched immediately to Camp Furlong in Columbus. General John J. "Black Jack" Pershing then led a military expedition into Mexico in pursuit of Villa. It was a campaign of firsts: the first time cavalry forces used motorized trucks and motorcycles, and the first time the Army used its new Aero Squadron for aerial observation and scouting. A young George Patton took part as well, and it was here that he began developing the ideas that would shape his later strategy for tank warfare. The machine gun on display defended Columbus during the raid; it was buried for safekeeping, rediscovered in the 1960s, and brought here for display.
