The doll and toy room is unique as we have toys made from the pioneers settling in Luna County and from Auburn Rubber the largest producer of rubber and vinyl toys in American and probably the world. They moved their molds from Auburn, Indiana to Deming in 1959–1960 timeframe. We have a doll from the Pancho Villa Raid of March 9, 1916. The doll belonged to the daughter of the telephone operator who called in the raid. Glass shards rained on the daughter's head as bullets broke the window above the area where she was sleeping. We have a Japanese doll from the ashes of Hiroshima. The hair is real from a Japanese woman. An American soldier picked the doll from a pile of rubbish and later donated it to our museum. A walk through this room will bring back childhood memories of toys you may have likely played with as a child. This room and the adjoining Old Timers' Room you just walked through were originally a small-caliber indoor shooting range used by the soldiers stationed here. During World War II, when this building served as a USO, these two rooms were converted to a bowling alley.
