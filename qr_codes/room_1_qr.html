You have entered the Old Timers' Room of our museum. The photographs displayed here date back as far as 1881, when Deming was founded. In the early days of the railroad, the Santa Fe and the Southern Pacific railroads drove a silver spike just outside Deming to complete the second transcontinental railroad line. Steam locomotives could only travel 60 miles before they needed to refill their water tanks. Deming is 60 miles from Las Cruces; Lordsburg is 60 miles from Deming; and Silver City is 60 miles from Deming. Deming served as a water stop for those early railroad locomotives. Deming sprung up very quickly once the water stop was established. Sometime around 1900, some of the Deming businessmen started an annual tradition of having a lavish dinner party. They called themselves "The Old Timers". They had a growing collection of photographs that they would display in the windows of Mahoney's Hardware Store. That photograph collection is now on display in this room. There is also a list of the Old Timer's Association Presidents for the last 113 years. The wrought iron photo racks were built sometime in the early 1980s by Harold Measday. To this day, the Deming Old Timers meet every October with class reunions, a banquet and a dance. The Old Timer's celebration is the longest continuing celebration in the state of New Mexico. Of particular interest in the wrought iron photographs are: The Deming Harvey House Holy Cross Sanitarium Other notable photos in this room are: Framed picture of the big fire dated July 11, 1918 Deming High School class of 1932 yearbook Picture of Paul and Alma Feil, local doctor 1918 diploma of Frances Isabel Phillips Faded picture labelled "1902 picture of Horse & Buggy Days" Herman Perez age 112 in 1928 Artist's rendition of Deming in 1898 with a view looking south on Gold Street and the railroad station
