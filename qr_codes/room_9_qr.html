📖 Audio Speech Narration Script 1:35

Deming was founded as a railroad town that soon expanded into a booming agriculture hub for farming and ranching. Ranchers would spend days, even weeks riding the range in all elements of our environment, tending to their herds. To get the job of branding and gathering cattle for shipping on the railroad, packing the right equipment and supplies was crucial. The chuckwagon on display is from the historic Diamond A Ranch located in Hachita, New Mexico, and was built from multiple parts of other wagons. Many meals were served from this wagon to hungry cowboys; cast-iron pans, Dutch ovens, a boiling pot, water barrel, and branding irons were carefully loaded aboard. Staples consisting of flour, cornmeal, pinto beans, green coffee beans, salt pork, beef jerky, and bacon sustained crews on long cattle drives. This chuckwagon was last used on a drive in 1972. One of the most important parts of a rancher's comfort is his saddle—the majority of saddles on display were made locally in the early 1930s and 1940s by Ruebush Saddlery, located on Silver Street until the late 1970s. The cowboy hats were donated by local ranching families; it was said that the distinctive crease, brim, and tilt of a hat let cowboys recognize each other from far across the range.
