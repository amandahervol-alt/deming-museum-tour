Ground broke on this armory in 1915 — and just two months after Pancho Villa's infamous raid on nearby Columbus, New Mexico, in March 1916, construction was complete. It holds a special distinction: the first armory built in New Mexico after statehood in 1912, and today, it's the oldest armory still standing in the state. Over the decades, the building has worn many hats. It served as a community social hub, then transformed into a bustling USO activity center during World War II, offering troops a place to relax and connect. In 1977, the Luna County Historical Society saw its potential and purchased the armory for $11,500, turning it into a museum. The spark that set everything in motion? A single donated item — Deming's first electric washing machine — which arrived at the museum's original home, a modest little house with no room to spare. That one washing machine changed everything. It set off a chain of expansion that transformed a cramped house into the sprawling museum you can explore today: 25,000 square feet, 47 exhibits, two stories, and more than 85,000 items on display. The Historical Society now spans three buildings — the Museum, the Archives, and the Custom House, which is available for party rentals. Don't miss the Gift Shop on your way out, stocked with mementos and information on the Mimbres Indians, military history, and other Southwest treasures. Admission is free, but donations are always appreciated.
